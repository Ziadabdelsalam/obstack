## 1. Q1 — People and the weekly rotation (the fence's shape, exactly)

- **D598 — a responder is a MEMBER of the workspace's organization; the product invents no people.** A rotation slot holds a `"user".id`, validated at write time against `member` rows of the session's org (`listOrgMembers`'s join, `invites.ts:222`); the read path LEFT JOINs `"user"` and renders an id whose row is gone as `(no longer a member)` (D13) — which cannot happen today (D148: no removal path exists) and is handled anyway, because "cannot happen" is not a column constraint. The mock's Nour/Salma/Omar are fixtures and stay in the mock; the live page shows the org's members by the name and email `"user"` holds (`0003_auth.sql:36`). A member may hold every slot of a rotation (a one-person team on call for itself is honest, and it is the drive's first shape).
- **D599 — the rotation object IS the mocked weekly shape and nothing wider.** `oncall_rotations`: `name` (UNIQUE per workspace, the definition-table idiom), `timezone` (an IANA name), `handoff_hour` (0–23), `primaries` (JSONB, EXACTLY seven user ids, Monday-first — the CHECK is `jsonb_typeof = 'array' AND jsonb_array_length = 7`), `secondary_user_id`. The day boundary is the handoff hour in the rotation's zone: the primary "for Tuesday" holds from Tuesday `handoff_hour:00` to Wednesday `handoff_hour:00`. The mock's `until Mon Aug 11, 09:00` is that boundary, computed. No start date, no rotation period, no layers: a week that repeats is what the mock draws, and what the fence names.
- **D600 — "now on call" is ONE definition with two implementations and a shared fixture (the D483 parity pattern), and the ingest image gains zoneinfo.** `onCallAt(rotation, nowMs)` in TS (`Intl.DateTimeFormat` with `timeZone` — the runtime carries full ICU) and `OnCallAt(rotation, now)` in Go (`time.LoadLocation`); a fixture of `(instant, timezone, handoffHour) → weekday, untilIso` cases — a DST transition, a zone east and west of UTC, midnight and the handoff hour itself on both sides — that BOTH test suites must reproduce identically. The image is distroless with no `/usr/share/zoneinfo` (measured), so the binary imports `time/tzdata` (one line, ~450 KB, the Go-blessed answer); the web tier validates a timezone on write with `Intl.supportedValuesOf("timeZone")`, Go re-validates on claim and treats a zone it cannot load as a step it cannot run (D608's fail-open, counted) — never a crash, never a guess at UTC.
- **D601 — the fence, restated as absences on the page:** no overrides, no swaps, no second layer, no calendar sync, no export. Pre-registered, USER-VISIBLE for a later run: one-off overrides (`from, to, userId`) as a second table read by the same formatter.

## 2. Q2 — Escalation policies and routing

- **D602 — a policy is an ordered list of one to five structured steps; the prose is presentation.** `steps` JSONB, the D481 shape: each `{ after: minutes, target }` where a target is a CHANNEL (notify it), or the PRIMARY or SECONDARY of a rotation (page them — §Q3). `after` is the delay measured from the PREVIOUS step's execution (from the escalation's opening for step 0), so the mock's `page primary → 5 min no-ack → page secondary → 15 min no-ack → notify #incidents` is `[{0, primary R}, {5, secondary R}, {15, channel C}]`. Validation is strict on both sides and frozen by a shared fixture of valid + invalid documents (the D483 artifact, second edition): exact keys, integer minutes in `0..1440`, one to five steps, every referenced rotation and channel resolving in the WORKSPACE (a database question, checked after the lock — `alerts.ts:388`'s split). A `fallback_channel_id` is REQUIRED on every policy (§Q3 D606: where a page lands when the person paged has no pager of their own).
- **D603 — routing is one nullable column: `alert_rules.policy_id`, `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, refused at the action layer while referenced — the channel precedent verbatim (`0010_alerts.sql:30-35`).** The mock's routing table maps a rule to a policy AND to channels; v1 keeps the rule's ONE channel exactly as S7.1 built it (the first delivery of every transition is unchanged — D484/D491 untouched) and adds the policy as what happens NEXT if nobody acknowledges. The "Channels" column of the routing table therefore renders the rule's own channel, read from the row it always had. **SLOs are not routed in v1:** an SLO breach emits `critical` (`slo_evaluator.go:238`) and rides the feed, but `slos.policy_id` is pre-registered, not built — the fence names alert rules, and an SLO's transition already carries its own channel (D511). Flagged (b).
- **D604 — an escalation OPENS on a rule's `ok → firing` transition at severity `critical` when the rule has a policy, and RESOLVES on the rule's `firing → ok`.** Both in the evaluator's own transaction (`evaluateRule`, `evaluator.go:321-365`): the row that writes `fireSQL` + the event also inserts the escalation (D607), and the row that writes `resolveSQL` marks the rule's open escalations `resolved` (the condition cleared; paging a person about an alert that is no longer firing is the mock's "30 min unresolved" read backwards). `warning` and `info` events never escalate in v1 — the phase plan's clause is "unacknowledged CRITICAL events"; a per-policy minimum severity is pre-registered. Flagged (c).

## 3. Q3 — Paging a person through the notifier that exists

- **D605 — a page is an `alert_events` row, and the deliverer delivers it unchanged.** When the walker runs a step it INSERTS a `pending` event: `rule_id` = the escalated rule (so the feed, the payload's `rule` key and the incidents promotion all keep working), `channel_id` = the step's resolved target, `severity` = the rule's, `title` = `"<rule name>: paging <responder> (<primary|secondary>, <rotation>) — step 2 of 3"` or `"<rule name>: escalated to <channel> — step 3 of 3"`, `detail` = the original event's detail plus the one sentence `Acknowledge at /app/oncall`, `escalation_id` set (a new nullable column, D610). `claimPendingSQL` gains one LEFT JOIN (the S7.3 D512 precedent) so `payloadFor` can add the additive key `escalation: { step, of, responder: { name } | null, rotation: string|null, opened_at }` (`payload.go:39-51`'s "by addition only"). The notify package stays credential-blind and fence-complete: "the notifier consults routing/rotation to pick delivery targets" (the run-goal) is satisfied one layer up, where the WALKER resolves the target and writes the row — the notifier never learns what a rotation is. Rate cap, one attempt per tick, three attempts, `delivered`/`failed` truth: all D490's, all unchanged.
- **D606 — a person is paged through a channel they OWN, and a person with none is paged BY NAME on the policy's fallback.** `notification_channels` gains `owner_user_id TEXT NULL REFERENCES "user"(id) ON DELETE SET NULL` (a channel outliving its owner becomes an ordinary workspace channel rather than vanishing under a rule that RESTRICTs on it — the only action that neither wedges nor destroys) and a partial UNIQUE `(workspace_id, owner_user_id) WHERE owner_user_id IS NOT NULL` — one pager per member per workspace. `setPersonalChannel` is the member's own mutation: it creates (or repoints) a channel of a v1 KIND — a webhook (an ntfy topic, a Pushover/pager service endpoint, a personal Slack channel's incoming webhook) — through the existing `createNotificationChannel` path, so it is masked, fenced and testable with `sendTestNotification` like every other channel. A `primary`/`secondary` step resolves to the responder's pager if they have one, else to the policy's `fallback_channel_id`, and the title NAMES the person either way: the mock's "the right person gets the page" is honest in both cases, and the second case is stated on the page (`Nour has no pager — pages fall back to #incidents`). No mobile push and no email exist here (the fence, D486); a webhook is how pager services take a page. Flagged (a) — the one item that prices this at the top of M.

## 4. Q4 — The walker, the state machine, acknowledgement

- **D607 — `oncall_escalations` is the walk's durable state, snapshotting the policy at open time.** Columns: `id` (`esc_` + 16 hex), `workspace_id` (FK CASCADE), `event_id` (FK `alert_events` **ON DELETE CASCADE** — an escalation of a swept event is meaningless, and CASCADE is the one action that lets the retention sweep's batch DELETE (`retention.go:158-165`) proceed; RESTRICT or NO ACTION would wedge that workspace's sweep forever — D526's argument, applied before it bites), `rule_id` (FK CASCADE), `policy_id` (FK **ON DELETE SET NULL** — display only), `steps` (JSONB, the policy's steps COPIED at open time — D491's "captured at emit" for the same reason: a later policy edit never retargets a walk in progress), `step_index` (the NEXT step to run), `next_step_at`, `state CHECK IN ('open','acknowledged','resolved','exhausted')`, `acknowledged_by` (`"user".id`, nullable), `acknowledged_at`, `closed_at`, `created_at`. Partial index `(next_step_at) WHERE state = 'open'` — the claim query's shape, the 0010 idiom.
- **D608 — the walker is a third claim loop in `internal/alerting`, on the deliverer's cadence, replica-correct by claim.** `EscalateDue(ctx)`: `SELECT … FROM oncall_escalations WHERE state = 'open' AND next_step_at <= now() ORDER BY next_step_at LIMIT $1 FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (workspace-blind by design, the D478 statement — it schedules, it reads no telemetry); per row: resolve `steps[step_index]`'s target (a channel; or the rotation's primary/secondary at `now()` through the Go `OnCallAt`, then that person's pager or the fallback), insert the page event (D605), then `step_index + 1` and `next_step_at = now() + steps[step_index+1].after` — or `state = 'exhausted'`, `closed_at = now()` when no step remains. Fail-open (D480's posture): a rotation whose zone cannot load or whose slot names a vanished user pages the FALLBACK channel with the reason in the detail, and counts `obstack_ingest_oncall_steps_failed_total`; nothing invents a responder. The ticker: 5 s, wired in `main.go` beside the deliverer with the same ordered shutdown; the drive and the Go tests call `EscalateDue` directly. A page ridden by a rule that is `firing` for hours is paged ONCE per step — the walk ends at `exhausted`, and re-paging on a schedule is pre-registered, not built (the D484 "re-notify policy v1 = none" reading).
- **D609 — acknowledgement is one click by any member of the workspace, in the product, and it ends the walk.** `acknowledgeEscalation(id)` (lock-first, D429): `state = 'acknowledged'`, `acknowledged_by = session.userId`, `acknowledged_at = now()`, refused unless the row is `open` (an acknowledgement of a resolved or exhausted walk is a no-op the page states, not a 500). The walker never claims a non-`open` row, so a step whose `next_step_at` has passed by the time the click lands is simply not run — proven RED in the Go integration test (a 1-second step, an ack between ticks, no second page). Any member may acknowledge (the fence says in-product, not "the paged person only"); who did is rendered. Where: the live page's **Open pages** section lists the workspace's `open` escalations (rule, step of count, next step in, the responder being paged) with the button, and the last twenty closed ones under it; the `/app/alerts` feed gains one chip on rows that belong to an escalation (`escalating · step 2/3`, `acknowledged by Nour`), linking to `/app/oncall` — no button there (the alerts surface's contract stays S7.1's plus one additive column). Flagged (d).
